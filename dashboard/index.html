Go deeper — how the sync works

One clock rules everything: the audio clock. Cues are scheduled ahead on that clock (a 25 ms lookahead, 125 ms horizon) — never polled, never guessed. Each recording's own first breath of sound was measured from the file itself and the map re-zeroed to it, so the light answers the sound you actually hear. Two listeners watch the sound: a fast one (0.05 smoothing) drives flashes and brightness; a slow one (0.30) drives only the ambient glow — the single smeared listener was V2's delay. At the navel, heart and brow the sweep pauses for about a third of a second at the knot — that pause is meaning, not lag, and the panel names it when it happens.

1 · The Outer Walls — bhūpura, the earth square

A square wall with four gates — the threshold between the world outside and the world within.

More

The bhūpura (earth-square) is the outermost veil: three lines, four T-shaped gates at the middle of each side. In this figure it is drawn in gold tracery only — gold is edging here, never paint. The wall carries no letters.

2 · The Sixteen Petals — ṣoḍaśadala

Sixteen petals carrying the sixteen vowels of Sanskrit, beginning at the east and running counter-clockwise.

More

अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ऋ ॠ ऌ ॡ ए ऐ ओ औ अं अः — the vowels (svara) are read as the energies of the moon's sixteen digits. Each glyph sits at its petal's centre; the first stands due east. Read them aloud at arm's length — that is one of this build's acceptance tests, and it is run on the real phone, not in a sandbox.

3 · The Eight Petals — aṣṭadala

Eight petals, deliberately bare.

Why are they bare?

Honest answer: the sources disagree. Some traditions place letters on these eight petals; others leave them empty, and the council's scholarship could not settle it. So this ring carries no letters, and says why, rather than inventing an answer. The ring stays blank in every state of the figure — rest, ascent, descent, crown, all three voices.

4 · The Fourteen Points — caturdaśāra

Where the great triangles cross, fourteen small triangles appear — the first woven field.

More

Four triangles point up (fire, Śiva), five point down (water, Śakti). Their crossings weave 43 small chambers in all. The crossings where three lines meet at a point of pressure are the marma — 18 of them, with 13 apex contacts — counted and published in the geometry panel below so anyone can check the figure independently.

5 · The Outer Ten Points — bahirdaśāra

Ten points in the outer ring of the woven field.

More

Each veil inward is drawn by the same nine triangles — nothing new is added; the eye simply reads a finer weave. That is the figure's whole method: one construction, many depths.

6 · The Inner Ten Points — antardaśāra

Ten points in the inner ring, one step from the heart of the figure.

More

The two ten-point rings together frame the eight-point ring inside them. In the sung cycle, this is roughly where the voice turns from climbing to returning — the descent is drawn at full brightness, never dimmed: the way back is not a fading.

7 · The Eight Points — aṣṭakoṇa

Eight points, the last woven ring before the centre.

More

Inside this ring stands a single triangle. Everything outside it is mixture; inside it, the figure becomes one gesture.

8 · The Central Triangle — trikoṇa

One downward triangle at the centre — the seat of the point of light.

More

The centre glyph you see during a recording lives here: लं वं रं यं हं ॐ — the seed-sound (bīja) of whichever centre is singing. At the crown it gives way to the point alone, and silence.

9 · The Point of Light — bindu

A single point, placed not at the geometric centre but at the triangles' own centre of balance — the incentre, a hair above middle.

More

The bindu sits at [0, +0.0055] of the radius — measured from the solver's exact construction, not placed by eye. When a recording reaches the crown, every colour drains, the point turns pearl, one soft flare of rays opens — and there is silence.

The Seven Centres — the body the sound climbs

Six sounding centres and the silent crown. Each card: the seed-sound, the element, the lotus, and what tradition says lives there.

What about the smaller centres?

Traditions also name smaller centres between these seven. The sources bound into this build do not name them with an attribution we can stand behind, so they are not listed — named only when a source can be cited, never invented.

About the colours

Two colour languages live here, and they are never mixed silently. Traditional: each centre's own element-colour and lotus-colour, from the Sanskrit sources. Modern rainbow (20th c.): red rising to violet — a Theosophical scheme (Leadbeater, 1927), beautiful and useful, but not a Sanskrit-source scheme, and labelled so wherever it appears. The light itself always speaks the spectrum — saturation lives in the light, not in painted surfaces.

The Three Knots — granthi, where the climb hesitates

At the navel, the heart and the brow, the rising energy meets a knot. In the music, the sweep of light pauses there — about a third of a second — on purpose.

More

Brahmā granthi — the navel knot, attachment to the material. Viṣṇu granthi — the heart knot, attachment to feeling and bond. Rudra granthi — the brow knot, attachment to the mind's own powers. The pause you see is scheduled on the audio clock as a plateau in the sweep — labelled here and in the figure panel, so it is never mistaken for lag.

The Five Sheaths — kośa, the layers of a person

Tradition wraps the self in five sheaths, food to bliss. The dashboard's layers mirror them: glance, detail, depth.

The five

Annamaya — the food sheath, the body. Prāṇamaya — the breath sheath, energy. Manomaya — the mind sheath, thought and feeling. Vijñānamaya — the discernment sheath, understanding. Ānandamaya — the bliss sheath, the innermost quiet. Named here as the tradition names them (Taittirīya Upaniṣad); nothing added.

The Breath — twelve seconds, in and out

At rest the figure breathes: in for about seven seconds, a held moment, out for about five — and the glow breathes with it, on the same envelope.

More

The breath is deliberately not a sine wave: inhale 6.9 s, a 0.3 s hold at the top, exhale 4.8 s — twelve seconds in all, the figure swelling ±1.3% and the light ±15%. Only the light and the scale move; no words, borders or buttons shift by even a pixel. In Calm mode (or your system's reduce-motion setting) everything else stills and the breath alone remains, gentler at ±0.8%.
